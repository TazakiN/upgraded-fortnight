\begin{abstract}
	Electronic medical records (EMRs) require access control that preserves patient authority while supporting collaborative healthcare. DecMed, as a decentralized EMR framework, already uses IOTA, IPFS, and Proxy Re-Encryption (PRE), but still has three main limitations: access issuance depends on patient re-issuance, access policy remains coarse-grained and is based mainly on role and purpose, and EMR data are stored as large objects that are difficult to enforce according to the need-to-know principle. This work designs and implements an extension of granular access control on DecMed through three contributions. First, a Policy-Based Access Control (PBAC) model defines relations among actors, EMR objects, access rights, and access conditions, and is represented as Macaroon tokens with caveats. Second, off-chain EMR data are segmented by dataset category and function category so that policies can be enforced on clinical data units. Third, token attenuation supports delegation among healthcare personnel without direct patient intervention; derivative-token formation is mediated by the PRE Server so that the derived scope does not exceed the parent token. PRE serves both as a cryptographic mechanism that supports redistribution of decryption capability and as an enforcement point before encrypted data are processed. The prototype covers EMR segmentation, Macaroon tokens, enforcement on the PRE Server, IOTA Move smart contracts, and adaptations of the Patient Client and Hospital Client for outpatient and inpatient workflows involving medical record officers, nurses, physicians, laboratory officers, and pharmacists. Testing was conducted through functional scenarios \texttt{P-KF-01} to \texttt{P-KF-12} and nonfunctional tests \texttt{P-KNF-01} to \texttt{P-KNF-16}. Within the evaluated test scenarios and prototype boundaries, the tested functional and nonfunctional requirements are considered fulfilled.
\end{abstract}
